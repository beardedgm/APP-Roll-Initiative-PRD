# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Tabletop RPG game masters running live D&D 5e and Pathfinder 2e combat encounters. They use the app at the table (or over video) during a session, often under time pressure with players waiting on them. The primary job: track initiative order, monster/PC hit points, and turn flow while pulling up stat blocks and rolling dice without breaking the pace of play. Secondary jobs (between sessions): build and save encounters, manage a character/monster library, and share a read-only player view.

## Product Purpose

Roll Initiative is a combat tracker that keeps a GM's whole encounter on one screen so running a fight stays fast and legible. It exists because GMs juggle initiative, HP, stat blocks, and dice across books, apps, and scratch paper; this consolidates that into a single resizable workspace. Success looks like a GM resolving a full turn (advance initiative, read a stat block, roll an attack, apply damage) in seconds without hunting through menus, and trusting the tool enough to pay for cloud saves, custom monsters, and shareable player links.

## Brand Personality

Refined and premium. Trustworthy enough to justify a paid subscription; calm, confident, and uncluttered rather than loud. Three words: composed, credible, crafted. The dark-fantasy theming (gold on near-black, Cinzel display serif) sets atmosphere, but restraint carries it — the polish should read as a professional GM tool, not a costume.

## Anti-references

- **AI-generated slop**: purple gradients, gradient text, identical icon-heading-text card grids, tiny tracked-uppercase eyebrows above every section, ghost-card borders + soft wide shadows. If it looks template-generated, it has failed.
- **Childish / cartoonish fantasy**: bright primary colors, comic-style illustration, playful rounded everything. The theming should feel like a serious game's rulebook, not a kids' app.
- **Cluttered legacy-VTT density** (secondary): the overwhelming, crunchy "everything on screen at full weight" feel of older tools. Density is fine; undifferentiated density is not.

## Design Principles

1. **Speed at the table wins.** Every screen is judged by how fast a GM completes the live-combat loop. Aesthetics serve legibility-at-a-glance, never the reverse.
2. **One workspace, no hunting.** Keep the encounter coherent on a single surface; reach for progressive disclosure over new screens and modals.
3. **Restraint reads as premium.** A single gold accent, deliberate hierarchy, and quiet surfaces signal quality. Decoration that doesn't aid the GM is noise.
4. **Atmosphere through type and tone, not clutter.** The dark-fantasy identity lives in typography, color, and copy — not in heavy ornament or illustration.
5. **Trustworthy under pressure.** Clear system status, reversible actions, and honest feedback. A GM mid-session must never wonder whether something saved or what just happened.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA. Body text ≥4.5:1 against its (dark) background; large/bold text ≥3:1. Visible focus indicators and full keyboard support for the core combat loop. Never convey state by color alone (the player/monster/NPC red-green-blue coding and HP-bar colors need a non-color cue). Honor `prefers-reduced-motion` for any dice or transition animation. Used in varied lighting (dim play spaces to bright rooms), so contrast headroom matters.
